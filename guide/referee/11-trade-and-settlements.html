Vaarn trades by barter, and so does the system. There is no currency and no buy or sell button. Items carry a value; you settle deals and move the items.

What things are worth

  • Every item's sheet shows its Trade Value at the top. Tags such as Bejewelled or Ancient count, and Advanced weapons are worth double. On a weapon made since 0.3.0, the box holds the base weapon's value and the line under it shows the worth with its tags (In effect: 3); that line is the value. On an older weapon the tags are already in the number.
  • Some tags are worth more to particular buyers. The sheet then adds a line such as … to Faa Nomads.
  • Exotica show an XP Value instead. They are traded for experience, not goods (Player's Guide, chapter 7).
  • Creature, steed, pet and vehicle sheets show an Approximate Trade Value, worked out from their Level or Hull.

Making a trade

  • Drag an item from one sheet to another to move it. For a stack, you are asked how many. Players can move items only between sheets they own, and into Dropped Items; a trade between two players' characters needs you.
  • Give to another actor (the ⇄ icon, Referee only) moves any item, with a dialog to choose who gets it.
  • Chat records each move.

Remove what the party trades away, and add what they get, by hand.

Trade goods

Generate Trade Good makes a trade good in the Items directory. Its Trade Value is per lot, and its Quantity is the number of lots.

Each good also has a secret quality that only you see, in a GM — Quality box on its sheet. The box says what the quality does and whether it is already in the numbers; a better or worse price is not, so apply it when the good is sold.

A False good has a Sold — start the … -day clock button. Use it when the party sells one, and the Active Effects board counts down to when the buyer finds out.

Settlements

Generate Settlement makes a town by the book's Mapping a Settlement: dice dropped on a sheet become its seat of power, water source, major asset, major problem, notable buildings and landmark, joined by roads inside a wall.

The preview

The macro opens a preview of the town before anything is made:

  • Hover a place to see what it is.
  • Drag a place to spread the town out or bunch it up. The roads, wall and dwellings follow; nothing rolled changes. Put places back undoes it.
  • Change the Settings, or press New beside the seed, for another town. Defaults brings back the usual settings.
  • The name is built from the town's details and never repeats one already in your world. Type over it if you like.

Create settlement makes its journal and its map.

The journal

The journal goes in the Settlements folder, for you only. Its Overview page has the town's size, people, government and faith, its customs, its gathering places (where roads cross: a market, a courtyard) and its Local Value Fluctuations. If a founder already founded another town, the two Overviews link each other.

Each marked place has its own page with Make an NPC here. The major problem's page is yours alone.

The map

The map goes in Settlement Scenes. Players see the town's walls, roads, dwellings and gathering places; the places themselves start hidden. When the party learns of one, press Reveal on the map on its page, or click the eye beside its link on the Overview: its icon and name appear for everyone. The major problem is only ever your pin.

Follow-up rolls

Where a page names something the system can make, it has a Follow-up rolls button: a Drug Cafe makes its drug, a Jigsaw Courtier's House spawns the courtier, a Vault Entrance opens the Vault Settings Tuner, a Fortress building or a Banditry problem rolls that place into the page, and a faith such as a Titan Cult opens its faction.

Local Value Fluctuations

  • Prized goods sell for twice their value.
  • Undesirable goods sell for half.
  • Despised goods can't even be given away.

Item sheets keep showing the base value; apply these yourself while the party is there.

Settlements in a region

A region's Settlement is named in the region preview, and its page shows its overview, its location suiting the land around it. Build this settlement on that page makes the whole town, journal and map, matching the overview (chapter 8). A Holy Place set in a Settlement can build its town the same way.

Identifying Exotica

Players see an unknown Exotica as Unidentified Exotica. On its row you have two icons:

  • Ask for an INT save to work out what it does: choose who tries. On a pass it is identified; on a failure it must be appraised.
  • Identify it (appraised): for when the party pays for an appraisal at a settlement. It is identified at once.

Either way, chat tells everyone what it is.

Try it

  1. Run Generate Settlement, drag a place in the preview, and press Create settlement. Read the fluctuations on its Overview page.
  2. Open one of its places and press Reveal on the map; look at its map.
  3. Run Generate Trade Good, open the new item, and read its GM Quality box.
  4. Drag it onto a character's sheet.
