The Exploration Clock is how time passes outside combat. Everything that ticks, such as afflictions, sustained Gifts, efforts, hazards and timed conditions, ticks when you advance it. Only the Referee can move time.

Opening it

Click the hourglass, Exploration Clock, in the Token controls on the left. Players don't have this tool.

The window shows the time (Day N — HH:MM) and how many Exploration Turns have passed. Set Where to Vault or Desert; the buttons change to match.

In a Vault

Leave Roll an encounter check each turn ticked to roll an encounter die for every turn you spend. The Encounter die choice is yours: d6 normally, or the lowest of 2d6 or 3d6 for an encumbered or noisy party. The clock names who is encumbered, to help you decide.

If the party is in a vault made by Generate Vault, set Party location, under Where, to their level. An Encounter then names a creature from that level's encounter table and offers to spawn it (chapter 8).

Click the button for what the party does. Each spends one turn unless it says otherwise:

  • Travel to another location
  • Search this location, disabled while the party is in darkness
  • Listen at a door
  • Short Rest, which only spends the time. HP and rations come from each character's own REST buttons.
  • Run — ten locations, no detail
  • Travel without light, 3 turns

A card, whispered to you, lists each turn's encounter roll and what it turned up. For any other number of turns, type it in and click Advance.

The two Long Rest buttons spend a night. A secure campsite rolls nothing. Without one, a d6 is rolled and 1–3 means the party is surprised in the night. Healing is still each character's own Long Rest on their sheet.

In the desert

Desert travel is counted in days. Start the day rolls the weather, the day's encounter check and the Vigilance Die; chapter 4 covers it. The desert-only buttons are:

  • Push — double distance: a day passes, the party covers double distance, and every player character (one a player owns) gains an Exhaustion.
  • Camp a day and night: a day passes and all Exhaustion is cleared.

Longer spans

+1 hour, +8 hours and +1 day only mark time. Nothing is rolled, but everything that ticks still ticks. A very long advance stops after a day, so afflictions tick a day at a time; click again to go on.

Exhaustion

The clock lists each character's Exhaustion and warns when one more would kill them. Select their tokens, then:

  • CON save vs Exhaustion posts the call for a save. Players roll it from their sheets.
  • Apply to selected gives each selected character an Exhaustion.

Select the character's own linked token. The clock refuses an unlinked token, because nothing done to it would reach the character.

What happens when time passes

You don't need to track any of this by hand:

  • Timed effects that run out post an Ended: card.
  • Efforts that finish, such as attuning an Exotica or a treatment, post Effort complete:.
  • Ongoing afflictions, sustained Gifts and vault hazards post a card with the buttons to resolve them.

Darkness

Darkness is switched on and off from the Active Effects board (chapter 5). While the party is in darkness, the clock disables Search and reminds you: encounters always surprise, ranged attacks can't be made, and melee has disadvantage.

Vault hazards and traps

When a rolled result names a hazard, obstacle or trap, you get buttons on its chat card. The same buttons appear on journal pages that name one. Players don't see them. Target the tokens the hazard reaches, then click. Depending on the hazard, the buttons call for saves, apply damage, start a per-round or per-turn effect (a per-round one reports only while a combat is running), or place the hazard as an actor on the map. A hazard that keeps going posts under Vault hazards: each turn.

Try it

  1. Open the clock, set Where to Vault, and click Search this location. Read the whispered card.
  2. Click +1 hour and watch the time change.
  3. Set Where to Desert and look at the day buttons. Chapter 4 picks up from Start the day.
