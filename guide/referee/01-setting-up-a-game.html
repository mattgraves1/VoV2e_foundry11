This guide is for the Referee. It covers the tools players don't see: the compendiums, the settings, the clock, combat and the generators. Players have their own Player's Guide.

The first launch

Launch the world and log in as a Gamemaster. On the first launch the system fills its compendiums from the book and posts a notice saying what it built. Wait for it before opening them.

Make a Scene and activate it before anything else. A new world has none, and without one the system's tools below don't appear, players can't drop items, and there is nowhere to put tokens. Any Scene will do.

Lighting and vision are on by default in Foundry. A new Scene has Token Vision ticked, so a player sees only what their own tokens see, and Global Illumination unticked, so nothing is lit. Characters' tokens see what is lit and nothing else. Pick one, in the Scene's settings, Lighting tab:

  • No lighting at all: untick Token Vision. Everyone sees the whole map, and tokens' vision does nothing.
  • Daylight with line of sight: tick Global Illumination. Tokens see everything in line of sight, stopped by walls.
  • Darkness: leave both as they are and give the party lights. The vault Scenes that Generate Vault makes are set up this way.

As the Referee you see the whole map, at the Scene's darkness. Selecting a token switches your view to what that token sees, which in the dark can be almost nothing. Press Esc, or click empty map, to see the whole map again.

  • Bestiary, Pets, Steeds and Vehicles: creatures ready to drag onto the map.
  • Vaarn RollTables: the book's tables.
  • Vaarn Items: armour, weapons, codices, exotica, implants and gear.
  • Vaarn Macros: the generators (chapter 7).
  • Vaarn Player's Guide and Vaarn Referee's Guide.

Players see only the Player's Guide. Every other compendium is yours alone. To let players browse one, right-click it and choose Configure Ownership.

If the notice says token art is not installed, creatures use Foundry's default token image. The system works the same either way.

After updating the system, if something new doesn't appear for a player, have them press Ctrl+Shift+R once. A plain refresh can keep the browser's old copy of the system.

The macros, the items and both guides update themselves every time you load the world, so a new version of the system brings them up to date. That overwrites any change made inside the Vaarn Items compendium: keep Items you customise in the world's Items or in a compendium of your own. Items already on a character sheet are never touched. The creature and table compendiums are built only when they are empty. To rebuild one, right-click it, choose Toggle Edit Lock, delete everything in it, and reload the world. Do the same if the first-launch notice says the build had errors.

Players and their characters

  • Character creation. Anyone can click Create Character (Vaarn) in the Actors sidebar. A player's character is made by your client, so you must be logged in when they click Create Character. They own it once it's made, and Foundry's Create New Actors permission isn't needed. You can also run the creator yourself and pick the player in Assign to Player at the last step.
  • Assigning a character. Assign to Player makes the player the character's owner. To make it their default character as well, set it in their User Configuration. Until a player has a default character, Foundry opens their User Configuration every time they load the world.
  • Placing tokens. Players drag their own characters, and companions they own, from the Actors tab onto the scene they are viewing. Your client places the token, so you must be logged in; Foundry's Create New Tokens permission isn't needed. They can't place anything they don't own, or anything from a compendium. They remove their own tokens the same way, with the Delete key, and you must be logged in for that too.
  • Tokens. New characters' tokens are Friendly, show their HP bar to their owner, and have vision: a player sees only what their token sees, and in the dark only what a light reveals, so a dark Scene needs torches or light sources. They are also linked to the character, so damage and conditions on the map land on the sheet. A character made before this version may not be. Open its Prototype Token settings and tick Link Actor Data once.
  • Portraits and token art. Foundry keeps its file picker from ordinary Players, so a player can't change their character's portrait or open their token's settings. To let them, make them Trusted Players in User Management. To let them upload their own images as well, also tick Upload New Files for Trusted Player in Configure Permissions.

Stay connected during play

Players can't change creatures or cards they don't own. The system passes those changes, such as a player's damage to a monster, to your client, which applies them. So a GM must be logged in while players attack or press card buttons. If no GM is connected, the player sees a warning that the change could not be applied, and nothing happens.

Settings

Under Game Settings → Configure Settings:

  • Sheet Theme and Sheet Heading Font: the colours and heading font of the sheets. Each person picks their own; it only changes their screen.
  • Initiative system: one of the book's four options. Side-based is the default. A change takes effect from the next round.
  • Edit Factions and Edit Relationships: add your own factions, hide the book's, and set who counts as an ally or enemy. The book's own entries are never changed.
  • Edit Gambits: add, hide or reword gambits.

Your tools

Four buttons are added to the Token controls on the left. Like all scene controls, they only appear while a Scene is shown:

  • Exploration Clock (hourglass): time outside combat. Chapter 3.
  • Desert Weather (cloud and sun): the day's weather. Chapter 4.
  • Factions of Vaarn (flag): the factions and their relationships. Chapter 6.
  • Active Effects (checklist): every timed effect, affliction and reminder in play. Chapter 5. Players can open it too.

Dropped Items

The system makes a Dropped Items actor the first time you log in. When a character drops something, it goes there, and anyone can take it back out. You can rename it. There must be an active scene for players to drop things.

Try it

  1. Create and activate a Scene, then open the Bestiary compendium and drag a creature onto it.
  2. In Configure Settings, try a different Sheet Theme, then set it back.
  3. Make a character with Create Character (Vaarn) and assign it to a player.
